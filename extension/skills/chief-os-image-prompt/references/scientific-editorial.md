# Scientific Editorial

Explain a complex subject through precise scientific illustration and editorial information design.

## Scene

Place one dominant [SCIENTIFIC_SUBJECT] on warm-ivory archival paper in a spacious asymmetrical composition. For multiple ideas, use one coordinated study or specimen per idea, structurally accurate and recognisable.

Use [CONCEPTUAL_LENS] to reveal how the subject works. A restrained [EVIDENCE_SYSTEM] connects diagrams, measurements, annotations, samples or process traces to visible features through thin connectors. Subject, lens and evidence make one coherent argument without explanatory prose.

- **[SCIENTIFIC_SUBJECT]:** a natural form, material, artifact, mechanism or system whose visible structure carries the idea (branching, layering, flow, symmetry, growth, erosion, assembly). Describe form, material, orientation, scale and placement, and keep it identifiable. Example: a translucent cross section of branching coral, centred slightly left.
- **[CONCEPTUAL_LENS]:** "Use [VISIBLE_FEATURE_OR_BEHAVIOR] to reveal [IDEA_OR_PROCESS], showing how [STAGE] connects to [STAGE]." Example: use the dandelion's dispersing seeds to reveal how one idea propagates through a network.
- **[EVIDENCE_SYSTEM]:** a small connected set of explanatory elements (magnified details, sequences, taxonomies, scale markers, field notes, process arrows, samples, coordinates). Every element explains, never decorates. "Connect [SUPPORTING_ELEMENT] to [VISIBLE_FEATURE] with [CONNECTOR_TYPE], and use [SECONDARY_ELEMENT] to clarify [RELATIONSHIP_OR_CHANGE]." Use short labels only (numbers, symbols, specimen IDs, scale marks), honour the caller's text whitelist, and never show placeholders.

## Visual Treatment

- Precise illustration informed by botanical atlases, natural-history plates, field journals, cross sections or architectural drafting
- One dominant anchor and idea; asymmetrical editorial grid, strong alignment, generous negative space
- Warm ivory, cotton paper, parchment or soft off-white ground; muted sage, olive, dusty teal, slate blue, terracotta, ochre, charcoal and warm grey as supporting colours; one purposeful accent colour, about five percent of the image
- Delicate black or warm-charcoal linework (construction lines, measurement marks, crosshairs, dashed connectors, numbered references), never thick strokes
- Subtle tactile materials (paper grain, tracing paper, ink, pencil, watercolour bloom, glass, transparent overlays); diffuse daylight or museum lighting with soft natural shadows
- Depth from layered information, not dramatic perspective or glossy 3D; implied movement through growth, migration, circulation, feedback, emergence or decay
- Dense at close range, calm and readable at a distance; multi-subject compositions share one visual grammar

## Avoid

- Scientific decoration, unrelated motifs, competing focal points, disconnected specimens
- Corporate infographics, dashboards, presentation templates, SaaS styling, decorative cards, rounded UI panels, glassmorphism, pill labels, app iconography
- Perfect symmetry, rigid poster layouts, evenly tiled content
- Thick outlines, heavy arrows, filled icons, cartoon graphics, clip art, stock-vector styling
- Bright gradients, neon glow, cyberpunk, holograms, excessive 3D
- Dense annotation that overwhelms the idea or removes negative space; fake paragraphs, prominent illegible type, arbitrary equations, meaningless data
- Heavy grunge, plastic surfaces, harsh reflections, HDR, strong drop shadows
- Unrequested logos, watermarks, captions, publication names or decorative headlines
