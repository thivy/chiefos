# Scientific Editorial

Explain a complex subject through precise scientific illustration and editorial information design.

## Scene

Place one dominant [SCIENTIFIC_SUBJECT] on warm-ivory archival paper in a spacious asymmetrical composition. For multiple ideas, use one coordinated study or specimen per idea, structurally accurate and recognizable.

Use [CONCEPTUAL_LENS] to reveal how the subject works. A restrained [EVIDENCE_SYSTEM] connects diagrams, measurements, annotations, samples, or process traces to visible features through thin connectors and clear spatial relationships. Subject, lens, and evidence must communicate one coherent argument without explanatory prose.

## Visual Treatment

- Precise scientific illustration informed by botanical atlases, natural-history plates, field journals, cross sections, or architectural drafting
- One dominant visual anchor and idea; an asymmetrical editorial grid with strong alignment and generous negative space
- Warm ivory, cotton paper, parchment, or soft off-white as the primary ground
- Muted sage, olive, dusty teal, slate blue, terracotta, ochre, charcoal, and warm gray as supporting colors
- One purposeful accent color, limited to approximately five percent of the image
- Delicate black or warm-charcoal linework: construction lines, measurement marks, crosshairs, coordinates, dashed connectors, and numbered references; never thick strokes
- Subtle tactile materials such as paper grain, tracing paper, ink, pencil, watercolor bloom, glass, or transparent overlays
- Diffuse daylight or museum-gallery lighting with soft natural shadows
- Depth created through layered information rather than dramatic perspective or glossy 3D effects
- Implied movement through growth, migration, circulation, feedback, emergence, expansion, or decay
- High information density at close range while preserving a calm, readable silhouette at a distance
- In multi-subject compositions, use one shared visual grammar while varying specimens, diagrams, and evidence

## Placeholder Definitions

### [SCIENTIFIC_SUBJECT]

A natural form, material, artifact, mechanism, or system whose visible structure carries the idea: branching, layering, segmentation, flow, symmetry, growth, erosion, or assembly. Describe form, material, orientation, scale, and placement; keep it identifiable.

Examples:

- A mature dandelion seed head shown in profile, with several seeds separating into the air
- A translucent cross section of branching coral centered slightly left of the page

### [CONCEPTUAL_LENS]

Use the subject's real structure or behaviour to make a process, relationship, comparison, or change over time immediately legible.

**Template**

Use [VISIBLE_FEATURE_OR_BEHAVIOR] to reveal [IDEA_OR_PROCESS], showing how [STAGE_OR_COMPONENT] connects to [STAGE_OR_COMPONENT].

Examples:

- Use the dandelion's dispersing seeds to reveal how one idea propagates through a network, from origin to independent adoption.
- Use the coral's branching cross section to compare biological growth with resilient decentralized infrastructure.

### [EVIDENCE_SYSTEM]

Choose a small connected system of explanatory elements: magnified details, sequences, taxonomies, scale markers, field notes, process arrows, material samples, coordinates, or comparison diagrams. Every element must explain the subject, never merely decorate it.

**Template**

Connect [SUPPORTING_ELEMENT] to [VISIBLE_FEATURE] with [CONNECTOR_TYPE], and use [SECONDARY_ELEMENT] to clarify [RELATIONSHIP_OR_CHANGE].

Examples:

- Connect two circular magnifications to the seed's barbs and flight path with hairline leaders, then add a three-step dispersal sequence.
- Link numbered branch nodes to a compact resilience diagram using dashed paths and a small scale reference.

Use clear, short labels only: prefer numbers, symbols, specimen IDs, scale marks, or compact notation to paragraphs. Honour the caller's rendered-text whitelist; never show placeholders or prompt instructions.

## Avoid

- Scientific decoration, unrelated motifs, competing focal points, or disconnected specimens
- Generic corporate infographics, dashboards, presentation templates, or SaaS interface styling
- Decorative cards, rounded UI panels, glassmorphism, pill-shaped labels, or modern app iconography
- Perfect symmetry, rigid poster layouts, or evenly tiled content
- Thick outlines, heavy arrows, filled icons, cartoon graphics, clip art, or stock-vector styling
- Bright gradients, neon glow, cyberpunk effects, holograms, or excessive 3D rendering
- Dense annotation that overwhelms the primary idea or eliminates negative space
- Fake paragraphs, prominent illegible typography, arbitrary equations, or meaningless data
- Heavy grunge, plastic surfaces, harsh reflections, HDR lighting, or strong drop shadows
- Unrequested logos, watermarks, captions, publication names, or decorative headlines
