# Everyday Doodle

A witty mixed-media image: a photorealistic everyday object combined with a hand-drawn cartoon scene.

## Scene

Place one photorealistic [EVERYDAY_OBJECT] per item on a warm-light-ivory studio background, with accurate texture, scale, lighting and surface detail. The object becomes [CLEVER_PART_OF_THE_SCENE] in a hand-drawn cartoon scene.

A [CARTOON_CHARACTER] interacts with it through visible contact: holding, climbing, pulling, balancing or riding. Choose object, transformation and pose together so the interaction is physically coherent, humorous or meaningful, and understandable without text.

- **[EVERYDAY_OBJECT]:** a recognisable object whose shape, opening, handle or function supports reinterpretation. Describe material, colour, orientation and placement, and show all of it, photorealistically (for example, a red ceramic mug seen from the side with its handle fully visible).
- **[CLEVER_PART_OF_THE_SCENE]:** one feature reinterpreted as a surprising role that matches the character's pose and personality. For example: the mug handle becomes a Ferris wheel while the character sits in its lower curve and waves.
- **[CARTOON_CHARACTER]:** a single stick figure with a pose or action, an emotion and a posture, with personality from eyewear, hairstyle, accessory and clothing. Show the whole character against the same ivory background.

## Visual Treatment

- Photorealistic object, never redrawn or stylised. Photographic and drawn lighting and scale match so they read as one physical scene.
- All drawn elements use thin, uneven, slightly overlapping warm-black pen strokes with visible texture. Shade with short hatch marks only: no gradients, soft shadows or solid flat fills.
- Characters: round head, thin neck, minimal torso, slender wobbly stick limbs like quick pen strokes, no volume, anatomy or muscle. Small, sparse dot eyes. Monochrome except clothing in one warm light colour (mustard, terracotta, sage or dusty blue), drawn with the same linework and hatching.
- Multi-item compositions share one visual rhythm while varying objects, character details, poses and personalities.
- Omit optional details that aren't provided. Never show placeholder names or explanatory text in the image.

## Avoid

- Objects without a conceptual role, unrelated decoration, or multiple primary photographed objects per vignette
- Floating, disconnected or implausibly scaled elements, or interactions without visible contact
- Cartoon-styled photographed objects, volumetric characters, airbrushed drawn elements
- Heavy rendering, dense backgrounds, excessive colour, symmetrical poster layouts, or infographic devices (icons, charts, flow arrows, callout boxes)
- Unrequested captions, logos, watermarks, product labels or illegible text
- Duplicated objects, malformed hands, distorted anatomy

## Example

A photorealistic red ceramic mug stands upright on a warm-light-ivory studio background, seen from the side with its handle fully visible, its glaze and cast shadow in true photographic detail. The handle becomes a Ferris wheel: a quirky female stick figure sits inside its lower curve, one hand on the rim and the other raised in a delighted wave. She is drawn in thin, uneven warm-black ink with a round head, dot eyes and slender wobbly limbs, round glasses and a short blunt bob. Her mustard-yellow dress is the only colour on her, shaded with hatch marks. A ticket stub and a queue rope sit beside the mug in the same line weight. Render the label "Send the agenda to Priya" in clear ivory space beneath the scene, with the note "The operations review is at 9:00 AM Fri 9 Oct" below it in a smaller handwritten script, both warm-black. Hatch-mark shading only, balanced composition.
