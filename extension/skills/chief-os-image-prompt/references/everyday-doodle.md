# Everyday Doodle

Create a witty mixed-media image that combines a photorealistic everyday object with a hand-drawn cartoon scene.

## Scene

For a single concept, place one photorealistic [EVERYDAY_OBJECT] against a clean, warm-light-ivory studio background. For content with multiple distinct items, create one coordinated vignette per item and use one photorealistic everyday object in each vignette. Every object should be a recognizable real-world item, reproduced with materially accurate texture, scale, lighting, and surface detail.

Integrate the object seamlessly into a simple hand-drawn cartoon scene. The object becomes [CLEVER_PART_OF_THE_SCENE], completing a clear visual pun or conceptual transformation.

In each vignette, a [CARTOON_CHARACTER] actively interacts with its everyday object through a visible physical relationship such as touching, holding, climbing, pulling, balancing, riding, or reacting to it. Each interaction should feel humorous, meaningful, or surprising and should be understandable without explanatory text.

## Visual Treatment

- Photorealistic everyday object; do not redraw or stylize it
- Expressive warm-black ink linework, with every character drawn as a simple stick figure
- Thin, uneven, slightly overlapping pen strokes with visible pen texture
- Hand-drawn elements are shaded with short hatch marks only, never smooth gradients or soft shadows
- Warm light colors on clothings
- In multi-item compositions, use a cohesive visual rhythm while varying the everyday objects and cartoon subjects
- Vary character details, poses, and personalities across multi-item compositions

Each photographed object and its drawn illustration must feel like parts of the same physical scene rather than separate layers. Multi-item compositions must still read as one unified image.

## Placeholder Definitions

### [EVERYDAY_OBJECT]

A specific photorealistic object with a recognizable silhouette and a physical feature that can be reinterpreted by the cartoon scene. Choose the object based on its shape, opening, handle, texture, scale, or function. Describe its material, color, orientation, and placement. The entire object must be visible and remain photorealistic.

Examples:

- An upright yellow banana with its curved side facing upward
- A red ceramic mug viewed from the side, with its handle fully visible
- A silver whisk lying diagonally across the scene
- A wooden clothespin standing vertically

### [CLEVER_PART_OF_THE_SCENE]

The surprising role the everyday object plays in the cartoon world and the precise way the character interacts with it. Reinterpret one recognizable feature of the object as something else, such as a vehicle, structure, landscape, tool, costume, or creature. Match the idea to the character's pose, personality, clothing, accessories, and direction of movement. The transformation should be immediately understandable without explanatory text.

**Template**

The [OBJECT_FEATURE] becomes [IMAGINED_ROLE], while the character [PHYSICAL_INTERACTION], expressing [EMOTION_OR_STORY_BEAT].

Examples:

- The banana's curve becomes a skateboard ramp while the character rides down it with arms raised.
- The mug handle becomes a Ferris wheel while the character sits inside its lower curve and waves.
- The whisk becomes a hot-air balloon while the character hangs from its handle in a tiny basket.
- The clothespin becomes a crocodile while the startled character tries to hold its jaws closed.

### [CARTOON_CHARACTER]

A single quirky [GENDER] stick-figure character with [POSE_OR_ACTION], [EMOTION], and [POSTURE]. With quirky personality details include [EYEWEAR], [HAIRSTYLE], [FACIAL_FEATURE], [ACCESSORY], and [CLOTHING]. Show the entire character clearly against the same warm-ivory background.

Draw the character to these rules:

- **Construction**: a simple round head, a thin neck, a minimal torso, and slender, slightly wobbly stick limbs resembling quick pen strokes. Give the character no volume, anatomy, muscle, or body mass.
- **Line**: thin, uneven warm-black ink with visible pen texture, drawn as though quickly by hand.
- **Face**: small dot eyes, kept sparse.
- **Shading**: short hatch marks only. Do not use smooth gradients, soft shadows, or solid flat fills on the character.
- **Colour**: the character is monochrome except for clothing, rendered with [COLOR_STYLE] and given form by hatch marks rather than blended shading.

Omit optional details that are not provided. Never display placeholder names or explanatory text in the image.

Choose the character, object, and transformation together. The character's pose must make physical sense for the object's imagined role, and at least one point of visible contact must connect them.

## Avoid

- Objects with no clear visual or conceptual role in the scene
- Unrelated decorative objects or more than one primary photographed object within a single vignette
- Cartoon styling applied to the everyday object
- Disconnected, floating, or implausibly scaled elements
- Weak interactions with no physical contact or visual relationship
- Fully rendered, volumetric, or anatomically detailed character bodies; the drawn character stays a simple stick figure
- Smooth gradient shading, airbrushing, or solid flat fills on hand-drawn elements
- Heavy rendering, dense backgrounds, or excessive color
- Symmetrical poster layouts and generic infographic composition
- Unrequested decorative captions, logos, watermarks, product labels, or illegible text
- Duplicated objects, malformed hands, or distorted anatomy
