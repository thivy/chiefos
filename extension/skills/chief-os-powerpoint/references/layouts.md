# Slide Layout Library

Select a layout by communication purpose. Layout names are semantic instructions, not fixed decorative templates. Column spans refer to the 12-column grid in [design-system.md](design-system.md#master-grid).

## Selection by Purpose

| Task               | Layout           |
| ------------------ | ---------------- |
| Introduce          | L01 or L02       |
| Assert             | L03              |
| Explain            | L04 or L05       |
| Immerse            | L06              |
| Showcase visuals   | L07              |
| Emphasise metrics  | L08 or L09       |
| Prove with data    | L10 or L20       |
| Compare            | L11 or L12       |
| Sequence           | L13 or L14       |
| Structure strategy | L15, L16, or L17 |

## L01. Title slide

**Purpose:** Open the presentation and establish the topic, audience, and tone.

**Structure:**

- No standard content-slide title zone.
- Title occupies 6–9 columns and may be vertically centred or placed in the lower third.
- Optional subtitle, presenter, organisation, and date.
- Optional image uses `5 / 7`, `6 / 6`, or full bleed.

**Rules:**

- One title, maximum three lines.
- Keep metadata secondary.
- Do not add agenda content, multiple cards, or detailed claims.

## L02. Section divider

**Purpose:** Signal a meaningful narrative transition.

**Structure:**

- Large section number or eyebrow label.
- One section title and optional one-sentence framing statement.
- May use a solid colour, restrained image, or generous white space.

**Rules:**

- Use at major transitions only.
- No charts, tables, dense bullets, or multi-part diagrams.

## L03. Executive statement

**Purpose:** Deliver one important conclusion or recommendation.

**Structure:**

- Large statement across 8–12 columns.
- Optional supporting evidence line.
- Optional quiet visual motif occupying no more than one third of the slide.

**Rules:**

- The statement is the slide.
- Avoid cards and bullet lists.

## L04. Standard title and body

**Purpose:** Explain a focused idea with short supporting content.

**Structure:**

- Standard slide title.
- Full width body, or `8 / 4` with a small supporting visual or callout.

**Rules:**

- Use three to five concise points.
- Prefer short paragraphs or labelled statements over nested bullets.
- Maximum two bullet levels.

## L05. Text and image

**Purpose:** Pair explanation with visual evidence or atmosphere.

**Structure:**

- `5 / 7`, `7 / 5`, `4 / 8`, or `8 / 4`.
- Image fills the visual region and is cropped proportionally.
- Text uses a short heading or assertion plus up to three supporting points.

**Rules:**

- Pick the split based on which element carries the proof.
- A decorative image must not receive more space than the message.

## L06. Full-page image

**Purpose:** Create immersion, establish context, or show visual evidence that needs maximum area.

**Structure:**

- Image fills the complete canvas.
- Text appears in a protected overlay zone, usually the left third, right third, upper band, or lower band.
- Optional translucent or solid scrim behind text.

**Rules:**

- Preserve the focal subject when cropping.
- Make sure text contrast remains accessible.
- Keep overlay text short: title plus one supporting line.
- Do not place text over visually noisy or semantically important regions.

## L07. Image gallery

**Purpose:** Compare or showcase two to four visual examples.

**Structure:**

- Two images: `6 / 6`.
- Three images: `4 / 4 / 4`.
- Four images: `3 / 3 / 3 / 3` or a 2 × 2 grid.
- Captions align consistently below or above images.

**Rules:**

- Use one crop ratio across peer images.
- Do not mix framed and full-bleed treatments within the same gallery.
- Every image must contribute distinct evidence.

## L08. Large number

**Purpose:** Make one metric memorable.

**Structure:**

- One dominant number using `large-number`.
- Unit is visually attached but smaller.
- One-line interpretation directly below.
- Optional supporting benchmark.

**Rules:**

- Use one dominant metric only.
- Explain why the number matters.
- Do not show a number without its period, unit, and context.

## L09. Metric row

**Purpose:** Show two to four comparable key metrics.

**Structure:**

- Two metrics: `6 / 6`.
- Three metrics: `4 / 4 / 4`.
- Four metrics: `3 / 3 / 3 / 3`.
- Each metric uses the same anatomy: label, value, unit, context, optional delta.

**Rules:**

- Use consistent precision and units.
- Highlight at most one metric as primary.
- Do not use card containers unless each metric is truly a separate entity.

## L10. Data insight

**Purpose:** Communicate the conclusion from a chart.

**Structure:**

- Standard assertion title.
- Chart uses 8–12 columns.
- Optional `8 / 4` or `9 / 3` split for chart and interpretation.
- Direct annotations are preferred over detached legends.

**Rules:**

- Choose chart type according to analytical task.
- Remove nonessential gridlines, borders, and effects.
- Highlight only the series or data point supporting the title.
- Include period, unit, and definition where needed.

## L11. Comparison

**Purpose:** Compare two alternatives, states, products, or approaches.

**Structure:**

- `6 / 6` for peer comparison.
- Shared comparison criteria align horizontally.
- Optional central divider or difference marker.

**Rules:**

- Compare like with like.
- Use the same visual order and information depth on each side.
- Mark a recommendation explicitly. Do not imply it by decorative styling alone.

## L12. Before and after

**Purpose:** Show a transformation.

**Structure:**

- `6 / 6`, with current state on the left and future state on the right for left-to-right languages.
- Optional arrow or bridge across the gutter.
- Each side uses matching anatomy.

**Rules:**

- Distinguish change using labels, structure, and content, not colour alone.
- Do not overload either side with implementation detail.

## L13. Process

**Purpose:** Explain a sequence of three to seven stages.

**Structure:**

- Horizontal for three to five steps.
- Vertical or serpentine only when six or seven steps require more room.
- Each step has a number, short verb-led label, and optional one-line description.

**Rules:**

- Connectors must show a real sequence or dependency.
- Use consistent step dimensions.
- Highlight one current or critical stage at most.

## L14. Timeline and roadmap

**Purpose:** Show milestones, phases, or outcomes over time.

**Structure:**

- Horizontal time axis using full width.
- Time intervals must be proportionate when dates are quantitative.
- Workstreams may use horizontal swimlanes.
- Optional upper band for outcomes and lower band for activities.

**Rules:**

- Separate milestones from ongoing work.
- Do not imply equal duration when dates differ materially.
- Limit to four swimlanes and eight milestone labels on one slide.

## L15. Three pillars

**Purpose:** Present three strategic themes, principles, or capabilities.

**Structure:**

- `4 / 4 / 4`.
- Shared baseline and consistent anatomy.
- Optional icon, short heading, one sentence, and one proof point per pillar.

**Rules:**

- Pillars must be peers.
- Avoid three unrelated cards presented only because there are three items.

## L16. Four-part framework

**Purpose:** Present four connected dimensions or operating elements.

**Structure:**

- `3 / 3 / 3 / 3`, 2 × 2 matrix, or quadrant.
- Select the form based on relationship: sequence, peers, or two-axis classification.

**Rules:**

- A quadrant requires meaningful named axes.
- Do not use a quadrant merely to arrange four items.

## L17. Matrix or quadrant

**Purpose:** Classify items against two dimensions.

**Structure:**

- Full-width 2 × 2 field with clearly named x and y axes.
- Labels sit outside the plotting field where possible.
- Items are placed according to evidence, not aesthetic balance.

**Rules:**

- Show directionality on axes.
- Do not crowd more than 12 labelled items into the field.
- Use direct labels instead of legends where practical.

## L18. Architecture or ecosystem

**Purpose:** Explain systems, components, layers, actors, and flows.

**Structure:**

- Full-width diagram.
- Use layers, zones, or `3 / 6 / 3` actor-system-actor composition.
- Arrange primary flow left to right or top to bottom.

**Rules:**

- Use no more than three hierarchy levels.
- Minimise crossing connectors.
- Use orthogonal connectors where practical.
- Label boundaries and trust zones explicitly.
- Icons support recognition but never replace labels.

## L19. Funnel or staged narrowing

**Purpose:** Show progressive filtering, conversion, or prioritisation.

**Structure:**

- Vertical or horizontal staged form.
- Every stage includes a value or explicit criterion.

**Rules:**

- Area must not imply quantity unless geometrically proportional.
- Prefer a stepped bar when exact comparison matters.

## L20. Table

**Purpose:** Present structured information requiring exact row-and-column lookup.

**Structure:**

- Full width or `10 / 2` with an interpretation sidebar.
- Header row is distinct.
- Numeric columns right-align. Text columns left-align.

**Rules:**

- Default maximum: 7 visible rows and 6 columns.
- Use 14–16 pt text where possible.
- Highlight the relevant row, column, or cell rather than decorating the whole table.
- Split dense tables across slides rather than shrinking text.

## L21. Quote or testimonial

**Purpose:** Humanise the narrative with a short attributed statement.

**Structure:**

- Quote occupies 7–10 columns.
- Georgia may be used for the quotation.
- Attribution and role sit beneath it.
- Optional portrait or contextual image uses `4 / 8` or `5 / 7`.

**Rules:**

- Use a short quotation only.
- Do not manufacture or paraphrase a quote as verbatim speech.
- Use quotation marks or a quote icon, not both at excessive scale.

## L22. Recommendation and rationale

**Purpose:** State a proposed action and why it should be accepted.

**Structure:**

- `4 / 8` or `5 / 7`.
- Narrow column: recommendation, decision, or call to action.
- Wide column: rationale, evidence, trade-offs, and dependencies.

**Rules:**

- Make the requested decision explicit.
- Distinguish facts, assumptions, and recommendations.

## L23. Risks and mitigations

**Purpose:** Present material delivery or decision risks.

**Structure:**

- Table, `6 / 6`, or three-column `risk / impact / mitigation` layout.
- Severity uses a label and icon in addition to colour.

**Rules:**

- Include owner or next action when known.
- Avoid decorative risk matrices unless likelihood and impact have defined scales.

## L24. Call to action and next steps

**Purpose:** Close a section or deck with action.

**Structure:**

- One decision statement.
- Two to four next steps using a short process, checklist, or owner/timing table.
- Optional contact or follow-up detail in the footer zone.

**Rules:**

- Name the action, owner, and timing where available.
- Do not end with a generic "Questions?" slide unless the user explicitly requires it.

## L25. Appendix or reference

**Purpose:** Carry supporting evidence that is useful but not part of the main narrative.

**Structure:**

- Standard title.
- Denser layout is permitted, but typography and accessibility minimums remain binding.
- Include reference identifiers, definitions, assumptions, or detailed tables.

**Rules:**

- Clearly mark appendix slides.
- Never use appendix density as permission for unreadable text.
