# Meeting Capture

Settle which meeting is being summarised, gather its evidence, then build the two collections the image renders.

## 1. Resolve the Meeting

Use the meeting the user attached or named; else the one they described that matches exactly one event; else list meetings that ended in the last 48 hours and ask one question. Never guess or summarise several. Stop if the meeting hasn't ended, was cancelled, or is still ambiguous. For a series, use the named or most recent completed occurrence and say which.

## 2. Collect the Context

Read what exists for that occurrence: recap or AI notes, transcript, meeting chat and linked thread, the calendar event, shared files, and post-meeting messages that reference it. Direct evidence beats generated summaries unless a later explicit correction supersedes it (recency alone isn't one). Report unresolved conflicts and gaps in chat; never assert a disputed decision or owner as settled. Use `the group` when agreement has no named speaker, keep names, numbers, dates and commitments exact, record an unsettled point as open, and skip anything personal, sensitive, off the record or that the user asked to exclude.

## 3. Build the Key Moments

A key moment is where the meeting changed direction: a decision, agreement, risk or objection, demo, reported number or status, scope or date change, or handover. Earliest first. Aim for 6–8 but keep the evidence-supported count (zero or odd included); never pad, split or drop to fit. Merge related or repeated points without losing facts and report merges. Exclude greetings, roll call, admin and inconsequential exchanges.

Each moment has: **Marker** (elapsed `m:ss` or `h:mm:ss` at source precision, else `Part 1`, `Part 2`; minute-only precision is `Minute N`; convert clock time only with a verified start), **Heading** (2–4 words), **Display label** (4–6 plain words on what changed, names and numbers exact), **Summary note** (one line, at most 12 words, exact names, numbers and dates), all rendered; and **Context** (full detail and who drove it), which guides the illustration only and is never rendered.

## 4. Build the Follow-Up Actions

A follow-up action is work a named person or the group agreed to do after the meeting. Order by when each was agreed. Aim for 2–4 but keep every distinct agreed action (zero, one or more than four); a discussed topic alone isn't one. Merge duplicates keeping the recorded owner and due date, and report merges.

Each action has, rendered: **Heading** (2–4 words), **Display label** (4–6 words, an instruction), **Summary note** (one line, at most 12 words, with the owner and due date as recorded, or `No recorded owner` or `No recorded date`); and **Context** (detail, who agreed, what it unblocks), never rendered.

Never merge unrelated items or shorten rendered fields below their minimum. The image reference owns layout and overflow, not which evidence survives.
